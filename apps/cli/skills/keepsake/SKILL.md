# keepsake

Run `keepsake <command> --help` for options. Output is JSON on stdout, progress is JSON lines on stderr, and errors are JSON on stderr, `{"error":{"tag","message"}}`, with exit 1, or 2 for invalid arguments.

keepsake only reads the folder of photos and never changes it. What it makes is in the `folder` that `keepsake shoots` prints.

## Before starting

- The photos are the user's private life, and keepsake sends them to the provider of the model, so use it only on a folder the user's own message names. Never copy photos or what they show into files, commits or issues.
- The vision model `codex/gpt-6.2-sol`, GPT-6.2 Sol through Codex, culls and picks the photos and crops them for the edits. The review model `claude/claude-opus-5-5`, Claude Opus 5.5 through Claude Code, prepares the edits by describing the chosen photos and marking people and signs, reviews the edits and explains what was left out. Each runs on the subscription its program is signed in to. That costs nothing extra but counts towards the limits of the subscription. When one is reached, the rest of the photos are listed as failed; run the same command again later. Both think with medium effort unless the name ends with another one, such as `claude/claude-opus-5-5@high`; it makes little difference. Whether a provider may train on the photos depends on the account's privacy settings.
- `--model` names another model to choose and crop, such as `claude/claude-opus-5-5`. It is needed only for `cull` or `run`; the later steps use the model that culled the shoot, once it has judged all but a few of its moments. `--review-model` names another one for the rest of `edit` or `run`.
- Everything else costs money. Editing costs about $0.75 per photo, and culling with an API key such as `azure/gpt-6-astra` about $2.50 per 100 photos. Scanning is free, so scan first, tell the user what the rest will cost, and go on when they have agreed to it or named a budget. `--budget` limits what the edits of one command cost and nothing else: keepsake sends a paid request only when what is left covers the most it can cost, and lists a photo it stopped for that under `overBudget`. `edit` and `run` refuse a model with an API key whose price keepsake doesn't know. Elsewhere such a model counts as free (`dollars` is 0 under `models` although tokens were used).
- If a command reports `NotSignedIn`, ask the user to run `keepsake login <provider>` in their own terminal (for Azure with `--endpoint <address of the resource>`), or to sign in to Claude Code or Codex. Never ask for the key in the chat, and never read or print the credentials file.

## From a folder to an album

The user can start an album on the page, or you with `keepsake run <folder> --title "<album name>" --about "<place, region, country, month and year>"`, which chooses the photos, edits them for up to `--budget` dollars, $25 unless you give another, and explains what was left out; `--publish` also uploads them. Add `--unedited` to stop before the edits, so the user can look through the choice on the page first. That costs nothing with the usual models, which run on the subscriptions, but money with a `--model` or `--review-model` that uses an API key. `--title` is required for a new shoot and names the album in English after the place or event the photos show, as precise as needed and without the date or the country, which Immich shows: "Mainau Island", "Sonia and Tom's wedding", or "Wilhelma" for a proper name without a common English form. `--about` tells the models where the photos are from, so always give it. When the trip was in another time zone than the camera's clock, which is the usual case when the time on the photos doesn't fit the light in them, give `--time-zone <such as Asia/Tokyo>` too, so the finished photos carry the time there. That works only for photos in which the camera recorded its offset from UTC; the others keep the camera's time. Run it in the background: 900 photos take about ten minutes to cull, and each edit takes a few minutes. When it stops for any reason, run the same command again and it goes on where it stopped.

The steps are also commands of their own, for a shoot that `scan` has named:

1. `scan <folder>` reads the photos and groups them into moments, such as a burst.
2. `cull` finds the best frames of each moment and scores it from 1 to 10. Models are generous: only a few photos get more than 8. Frames that show something clearly different from the best one are listed under `further`, each with its own score.
3. `pick` takes the best and the `further` photos of every moment from `--minimum-score` on, 6 unless you give another, and leaves out repeats, also of the same thing a few minutes later. With `--maximum` it compares them with each other and keeps the best, with a variety of places, people, animals and the rest. For an album of a certain size give it together with a lower `--minimum-score`, such as 5, so there is enough to choose from.
4. `edit` crops each pick to an interesting composition, with more room in front of an animal or a person than behind, which may reach a little beyond the photo where the photo cuts off a subject or what belongs to it, such as the flower a bee flies to, or leaves a subject no room; the image model continues the scene there. It finds what pulls the eye away, such as strangers in the background, cranes, building work, fences, nets, poles, bins or a chair at the edge, and has it removed, but keeps the subject, the people with them and what tells the place. The photo's page lists what was removed, as does its `removals.json`. It makes `--versions` of it, puts the original's people, changed signs and forests back into them, and has them reviewed. People who can be recognised keep their own pixels and get only the light of the edit, so their features can't change; small people in the background and in reflections, and animals too small for the image model to draw, such as birds far away, come back as patches. The versions that pass are inspected part by part at full size, and what is wrong, such as a melted person or woven grass, is put back from the original and inspected again. Where no version passes, it keeps the camera's own picture, listed under `keptFromCamera` with the reason.
5. `explain` says of every photo that isn't in the album why: what speaks against it, or what the photo that shows the same does better. Run it again after the album changed; it asks only about photos whose reason that touches. Put a reason the user gave themselves into `take-out --because`.
6. `publish` uploads the finished photos to an album in Immich, each stacked on the camera's own picture and tagged `keepsake`. Publish only when the user asks for it. A shoot's first publish makes a new album under its title, also when an album of that name exists. The album may be changed in Immich too: `publish` puts nothing back that was taken out there unless it is named. It goes on publishing to the same album, found by its id, also after it was renamed in Immich or when another album has its name. `--album <name>` publishes into another, an existing one of that name or a new one; when two albums have that name, it stops before uploading and says so, and only then ask the user to rename one.

`show <shoot>` says where a shoot stands and where the files are, and `show <shoot> <photo>` gives everything about one photo, with its `standing` and its `link` on the page. Look at a few finished photos yourself before you call them done, and at every one with people, close up and beside the camera's picture: their edges should look natural, with no halo and no second outline.

## The page

`keepsake serve` serves a page where the user looks through their albums: every photo with where it stands and why, every edit beside the camera's picture and the edits before it, and what it cost. They make an album out of a folder below one that `--photos` named, which the page gets ready with `run --unedited`, and mark favorites, which are Immich's. They take photos out of the album or the choice, put photos in, and use one instead of the photo in front of it, and the page does that itself: a photo put in that has a `final` goes into the album if the album exists, and one without waits for an order. They also mark what should change in a photo, or order the edits of the chosen photos that aren't edited yet, and hand that over, and the page carries that out itself too: one hand-over after the other in each album, within what they agreed to pay, and on again after a restart. It answers each mark with what came of it. Where it can't go on without them, such as when what they agreed to is used up or a subscription reached its limit, the page shows them what it waits for and lets them go on.

When you write to the user about photos, link to them on the page with the `link` that `show` and `marks` give, instead of naming files. A `link` is null while the page isn't served. Then it is for this machine only, unless something passes it on and `--address` says where it is reached from. Where it is set up as a service, restart that instead of serving it a second time.

When the user asks how far something they handed over is, `marks <shoot>` lists what the page is still carrying out: the marks with their `note`, `boxes` and the photo's files, `album` with the `photos` still to be put in when they ordered the edits of the chosen photos, and `agreedDollars`, what is left of what they agreed to pay for it. `--all` lists the answered marks too. Leave carrying it out to the page; link them to it instead.

When they ask in the chat for a mark to be carried out by you instead, `edit <shoot> --mark <id> --budget <what is left>` takes the note as the wish and changes only what is in the boxes, if there are any, and `publish <shoot> --mark <id>` puts it into the album. Give `--wish` when the note needs to be clearer. A `change` costs about $0.45, twice that when a place needs a second attempt, and a photo without a `final` about $0.75.

Only one command at a time works on a shoot; another one stops with `ShootBusy` and changes nothing. So publish after the edits have ended, and read what each command prints on stderr too, rather than hiding it. The page still takes photos out and puts them in while `edit` runs. `pick`, `publish` and `take-out` wait while it does, and the page waits for them: until they end, it shows the photo on its way.

Do the same when they tell you in the chat instead: `take-out` with their reason, so the page can show it. Photos that are only chosen so far can be taken out too.

## Changing the result

- Other or more photos: run `pick` with another score or maximum, then `edit` and `publish` again. Photos already edited stay. For photos that weren't picked, such as another frame of a burst, name them: `edit <shoot> <photo...>` and `publish <shoot> <photo...>`.
- A certain change to a finished photo, such as something to remove: `edit <shoot> <photo> --wish "<what the user wants>"`, then `publish <shoot> <photo>`, since `publish` without names leaves out photos that weren't picked. It changes only the places the wish is about and leaves everything else as it is, for about $0.45, or twice that when a place needs a second attempt. What didn't work is listed under `notDone` with the reason, and the photo stays as it was there. Look at the changed places close up, beside the photo before. A wish to have back something keepsake removed, also with a mark, is a change to all of the photo, since only the camera's picture still shows it. Say so when keepsake's own removals took away somebody the user may want back.
- A change to all of a photo, such as warmer light: the same commands. It edits the photo anew in the frame it has, with all earlier wishes, and the edit there is stays when none of the new versions passes.
- Another frame, such as more room around the subject: `edit <shoot> <photo> --again --wish "<what the user wants>"`, then `publish <shoot> <photo>`. It starts over, so the wish should say everything the user wants of the photo. `--again` without `--wish` keeps the earlier wishes.
- Another attempt at a photo: `edit <shoot> <photo> --again`, then `publish <shoot> <photo>`.

After each of these, and after a mark, `publish` moves the earlier upload to Immich's trash itself, since the user asked for the better one and not for two, and lists its id under `outdated`; the trash keeps it for a month. When that failed, `trashFailed` says why, and the next `publish` tries again.

## Comparing vision models

`keepsake rate <shoot>` asks the user on the page to choose the best photo of each moment, give it stars and mark others worth keeping, 60 moments of up to 12 photos unless `--moments` says otherwise. Give them the printed `link`. Never rate for them.

`keepsake bench <shoot> --model <provider/model> --model …` then asks each model about the rated moments, at the price of culling them (nothing extra on a subscription):

- `sameBestFrame` is how often the model chose a frame of a burst that the user would keep, and `bestFrameByChance` how often guessing would.
- `keepAccuracy` is how often a photo the user would keep (3 stars or more) scored above one they would not; 0.5 is guessing. Use this to choose a model.
- `rankCorrelation` compares the order of all scores with the order of the stars, from -1 to 1.

When two models come out close, let the user rate the moments those two judge differently. Run `cull <shoot> --model <model>` with both, last with the one that should stay the judge of the shoot, then `rate <shoot> --between <model> --between <model>`, and `bench` with both. Only the difference between the two says something then: both get lower numbers on these moments, and a third model looks better than it is next to them.
